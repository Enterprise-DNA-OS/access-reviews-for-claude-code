[ConductorOne's vendor listing on AWS Marketplace](https://aws.amazon.com/marketplace/pp/prodview-wrixbdsadifke) shows US$100,000 for a 12-month platform contract. This is a published contract offer, not evidence of a particular NZ or AU customer's bill, and not the price of this narrower review register. No customer use or invoice was confirmed in the available local customer/CRM material. Buyer fit is a hypothesis for professional firms with recurring access reviews, so that score stays low.

[Current C1 pricing](https://www.c1.ai/pricing) offers scoped platform pricing based on identities, level and modules, and usage pricing. It provides no generally applicable dollar price. The landing page therefore quotes no dollar amount. The AWS amount is retained here as selection evidence, not transplanted to a vendor-site price claim.

[Dovetail pricing](https://dovetail.com/pricing/) is free or a tailored enterprise quote. [SpendHound's benchmark](https://www.spendhound.com/marketplace/dovetail-pricing) reports an enterprise annual average above thirty thousand US dollars but a small-business average below three thousand. Those are third-party benchmarks, not a vendor price or local invoice. Media processing also makes the complete product a poor one-session fit.

SecurEnds has clear [campaign export documentation](https://www.securends.com/documentation/campaign-reports/). A search result on its staging hostname quotes an annual entry package, but its production pricing URL redirects to a dollar-sign image. Treat the staging figure as unconfirmed and do not quote it as a current offer. Its annual-bill score remains provisional, so it loses to ConductorOne.

Search results contained competing alternative lists for access-review products. No keyword volume was measured. Search-demand scores are qualitative. These are candidates, not additional queued builds.

[Manage campaigns](https://www.c1.ai/docs/product/admin/manage-campaigns) documents task CSV exports, reassignment, decisions, revocations and report downloads. [Create campaigns](https://www.c1.ai/docs/product/admin/campaigns) describes scoping, owners and review policies. The [official c1i repository](https://github.com/ConductorOne/c1i) documents CSV report generation. Those establish the import route. Exact tenant headings need explicit mapping, which is why import scores four rather than five.

The build owns certification records and evidence. It does not replace C1 identity discovery, live connectors, access enforcement, authentication, provisioning or its full governance platform. Managed work scopes those connections separately. We make no claim that the incumbent cannot answer our ten example questions: its reports and custom analysis can overlap.
